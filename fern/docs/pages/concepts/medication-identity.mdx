---
title: Medication identity
subtitle: Free text, SFRX IDs, qualifiers, and how a product is chosen
---

Safety checks evaluate registry **products** (a specific strength and form).
Every medication list across the API (`enterprise_registry_resolve`,
`enterprise_safety_check`'s `medications[]`, the hosted MCP's
`resolve_medications` / `check_medication_safety`) accepts the same entry
shape, and the API turns each entry into one product before anything else runs.

## Free text

Send whatever text you have — a prescription line, a patient-reported name,
an OCR-extracted string — as `input_text`. What is written is parsed and used
as evidence:

- **Strength**, with or without a unit (`"metformin 500"`), with a decimal comma
  (`"2,5 mg"`), as a combination (`"Augmentin 875/125"`) or an amount per volume
  (`"100 mg/5 ml"`). 1 g and 1000 mg are equivalent.
- **Dosage form and route**, stated or implied: `"Diclofenac IM"`, `"injection"`,
  `"amp"`, `"syrup"`, `"inhaler"`.
- **Salt** (`"metoprolol succinate"`) and **release type** (XR, ER, SR, XL, CR,
  MR, LA, retard, prolonged, extended, modified).
- Arabic units and digits (`"وارفارين 5 مجم"`), common synonyms and spelling
  variants (`"albuterol"`), and words in any order
  (`"trimethoprim sulfamethoxazole"`).

## Qualifiers as fields

`dose_text`, `route` and `dosage_form` carry the same information as separate
fields: `{"input_text": "Warfarin", "dose_text": "5 mg", "dosage_form": "tablet"}`.
They are **resolution inputs only**: they choose the registry product and are
not read by the safety evaluation. Fields the evaluation cannot use (for
example `frequency_text`) are refused with `422 enterprise.safety.request_invalid`
naming the field, rather than accepted and ignored.

## SFRX IDs

Once a medication has been resolved, you get back an `sfrx_id`
(format `SRX-NNN-NNNNNN`), a stable registry identifier. Passing `sfrx_id`
on a later call, alongside or **instead of** `input_text`, is authoritative:
the product is fetched directly, no text matching can replace it, and an
unknown id is reported as `sfrx_id_unknown` on that entry (a safety check whose
only target is unknown returns `422 enterprise.safety.target_resolution_required`).

Use `enterprise_registry_product_detail` with a known `sfrx_id` to fetch the
full product record directly, rather than re-resolving from text.

## A bare name: the default systemic variant

A name alone (`"Warfarin"`, `"Aspirin"`) usually matches one medicine in
several strengths, forms, packs or manufacturers. Rather than refusing, the
Enterprise API and the hosted MCP select a **default systemic variant** and run
the check: an oral tablet or capsule first, then other systemic forms, then a
product with a stated strength, then the registry's preferred listing, with a
deterministic tie-break. The result always says so:

- the entry in `medications[]` has `selection: default_variant`, or
  `equivalent_product` when the candidates were several **brands** of one clinical
  identity (same ingredients and salt, release type, strength, form and route),
  and `product` names what was evaluated;
- `review.reasons[]` contains `default_variant_selected`, asking the caller to
  confirm the product (pass its `sfrx_id`, or `dose_text` / `route` /
  `dosage_form`, to pin it);
- `review.required` is `true`.

This is conservative for ingredient-level domains (interactions, allergy,
pregnancy and lactation). For anything dose-sensitive, pin the product.

The same product listed in several **pack sizes** is one medicine: one listing is
selected with no review code, and the others are published in the selected
candidate's `equivalent_candidates[]`, each with an `equivalence_reason`.

## How a product is chosen: evidence, then elimination

A wrong product selected silently is worse than no selection, so resolution is a
sequence of explicit judgements:

1. **Find everything the name can mean**, under both the English and Arabic
   names, including close spellings.
2. **Judge each product against what was written**: strength, form, route, salt
   and release type are each matched, contradicted, or cannot be checked (the
   registry holds no value).
3. **Eliminate.** A product that contradicts anything written is never selected.
   Strength is compared on the whole set (`875/125` matches only 875/125).
   Salt, release type and route are part of a product's identity: products that
   differ in any of them are never treated as equivalent. Unspecified release
   means immediate release; a modified-release product is selected only when no
   immediate-release product fits, and then as `default_variant_selected`. A
   brand modifier is identity too: `Panadol Extra` never selects plain Panadol.
4. **Decide or say why not.** If one product (or one clinical identity) survives,
   it is selected. Otherwise nothing is selected and a reason code says which
   written fact no product has.

The candidate `confidence` only orders the candidates. It is not calibrated and
never decides a selection on its own.

## How the choice was made: `match`

Every resolution carries `match`. Its `basis` says how the selection was made:

| `basis` | Meaning | Review |
|---|---|---|
| `by_id` | Your `sfrx_id` named the product | Only if the text contradicts it |
| `exact_product` | The text pins one product | No |
| `exact_medicine_default_variant` | The medicine is certain; strength, salt, release or variant was chosen by the published preference | Yes |
| `equivalent_product` | One of several brands of one clinical identity | Yes |
| `offered_only` | Candidates offered, nothing selected | Yes |
| `none` | Nothing found | Yes |

For a selection, `evidence` says how the product relates to what you wrote, axis by axis
(`name`, `strength`, `form`, `route`, `salt`, `release`):
- `matched`: written, and the product agrees.
- `inferred`: not written; the product supplies it. This is the resolver's assumption.
- `unverified`: written, but the registry records nothing to check it against.
- `conflict`: written and contradicted. This happens only through an `sfrx_id` you supplied.
- `absent`: neither written nor recorded.

`strength_unit_inferred` names the unit assumed for a strength written without one
(`metformin 500` → `mg`).

### Measured precision

SafeRx measures the resolver on a golden set. Every line in it is written from a known
registry product, so its correct answer is a fact of the registry. On 2,367 lines from 398
medicines (Registry V181):

| `basis` | Selections | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| `exact_product` | 1,167 | 0 |
| `equivalent_product` | 249 | 0 |
| `exact_medicine_default_variant` | 148 | 0 |

- Misspellings one edit from a brand were offered 99.7 % of the time and never selected.
- Lines that name one product were selected 96.5 % of the time.
- The set measures the matching rules, not real handwriting or OCR. Rely on
  `review.required`, not on these figures, for a decision about a patient.

## Reason codes

Every item carries `review.reason_codes`; `review.required` is `true` whenever
any code is present. New codes may be added: treat an unknown code as "needs
review".

| Code | Selected? | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `default_variant_selected` | yes, review | The text did not pin one product; the published preference chose one, or a written fact could not be verified |
| `equivalent_product_selected` | yes, review | One of several brands of the same clinical identity was chosen |
| `missing_strength` | yes, review | No strength was written; the product's strength was assumed |
| `registry_field_conflict` | yes, review | The text contradicts the product of the `sfrx_id` you supplied; the `sfrx_id` wins |
| `ambiguous_candidates` | no, candidates | The text matches different medicines |
| `strength_not_available` | no, candidates | The medicine exists, but no product has the written strength |
| `form_not_available` | no, candidates | ... no product has the written dosage form |
| `route_not_available` | no, candidates | ... no product has the written (or implied) route |
| `salt_not_available` | no, candidates | ... no product has the written salt |
| `release_not_available` | no, candidates | ... no product has the written release type |
| `approximate_name_match` | no, candidates | Only a close spelling matched. Confirm the intended medicine |
| `ocr_readings_disagree` | no, candidates | The independent readings of a prescription line name different medicines or products |
| `lookalike_risk` | no, candidates | A read prescription line touches a known look-alike pair (for example hydroxyzine/hydralazine) |
| `unmapped_medication` | no | No registry candidate at all |
| `sfrx_id_unknown` | no | The `sfrx_id` is well-formed but no active product carries it. Its text never rescues it |
| `query_rejected` | no | The text could not be searched (it must be 2 to 80 characters) |

Treat every "no, candidates" code the way you treat `ambiguous_candidates`:
show the candidates and let a person choose. In a safety check, those
medications are `status: ambiguous` with `candidates[]` and a `reason` in words.

When the text names **different medicines** (two single-ingredient generics
match equally well), nothing is selected: the medication is `status: ambiguous`
with up to five `candidates[]`. If no new medication resolves, the safety check
is `422 target_resolution_required` and its body carries the same structured
`medications[]` so the caller can choose.

## What was evaluated: `medications[]`

Every successful safety check returns `medications[]`, one entry per request
medication in request order: `ref` (your `client_medication_id`, or `m1`, `m2`,
…), `role` (`new` / `current`), `input`, `status` (`resolved` / `ambiguous` /
`unresolved`), and for a resolved one `selection` and
`product{sfrx_id, name, generic, strength, form}`. Show it to the user: it is the
only way to know which product an interaction was computed against, and
`findings[].medication_refs` point back to these refs.

An entry whose text the registry cannot search (one character, or longer than
80 characters) is `unresolved` with a bilingual `reason`; the other medications
are still evaluated. A check needs at least one new (`target_medication`)
medication to resolve.

## Resolution on `enterprise_registry_resolve`

`enterprise_registry_resolve` takes 1 to 50 medications and returns one item
per medication with `candidates[]` — ranked possible matches, each with a
`confidence` (0–1, an ordering score, **not comparable across queries**) — and
the same selection rules. `selected_candidate_id` is the `candidate_id` of the
chosen candidate (read the `sfrx_id` from that candidate) when a product was
chosen by identity, by what was written, or by a published policy, in which
case `review.reason_codes` says so. When it is `null`, present the candidates
for the caller to choose rather than assuming the first one. An empty
`candidates` array means no match was found — it does not mean the product does
not exist; prompt for more specific input (dose, form) before concluding the
medication is unknown.

Malformed input is refused rather than guessed: more than 50 medications is
`422 request.collection_too_large`, and an `sfrx_id` that does not match
`^SRX-[0-9]{3}-[0-9]{6}$` is `422 request.sfrx_id_invalid`.

Prescription lines read by OCR are resolved by the same rules, with each
independent reading of the line as evidence; see
[OCR prescription lifecycle](/concepts/ocr-lifecycle).

## current vs. target medication roles

On `enterprise_safety_check`'s `medications[]`, each entry has a `role`:

- **`target_medication`** (default) — the medication under review.
- **`current_medication`** — a medication the patient is already taking,
  supplied so it's considered for interaction checking against the target.

Send every concurrently-taken medication you know about as
`current_medication` entries on the same request — Enterprise never reads a
saved list of current medications; it only sees what's on this request.

## Review behavior

Resolution results carry their own `review` state (`ReviewState`), separate
from a safety check's review state. Any reason code on an item means review is
required before you rely on the selected candidate — check `review.required`
and `review.reason_codes` on the resolution result, not just on a downstream
safety check.
