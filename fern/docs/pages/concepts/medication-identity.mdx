---
title: Medication identity
subtitle: Free text, SFRX IDs, and resolution
---

Medications can be identified two ways across the API, and most operations
accept both.

## Free text

Send whatever text you have — a prescription line, a patient-reported name,
an OCR-extracted string — as `input_text`. This is the right choice whenever
you don't already have a resolved registry identifier. Every medication list
input across the API (`enterprise_registry_resolve`,
`enterprise_safety_check`'s `medications[]`, the hosted MCP's
`resolve_medications`/`check_medication_safety`) accepts `input_text`.

## SFRX IDs

Once a medication has been resolved, you get back an `sfrx_id`
(format `SRX-NNN-NNNNNN`) — a stable registry identifier. Passing `sfrx_id`
on a later call (alongside or instead of `input_text`) skips ambiguous
free-text matching and resolves directly to that registry entry. On
`enterprise_registry_resolve`, `sfrx_id` takes precedence over `input_text`
when both are supplied.

Use `enterprise_registry_product_detail` with a known `sfrx_id` to fetch the
full product record directly, rather than re-resolving from text.

## Resolution ambiguity

`enterprise_registry_resolve` returns `candidates[]` — ranked possible
matches, each with a `score` (0–1, **not comparable across different
queries**). `selected_candidate_id` is populated only when resolution is
confident enough to auto-select one candidate; when it's `null`, your
integration should present the candidates for the caller to choose, rather
than assuming the first one.

An empty `candidates` array means no confident match was found — it does
not mean "no such product exists in the registry." Consider prompting for
more specific input (dose, form) before concluding the medication is truly
unknown.

## current vs. target medication roles

On `enterprise_safety_check`'s `medications[]`, each entry has a `role`:

- **`target_medication`** (default) — the medication under review.
- **`current_medication`** — a medication the patient is already taking,
  supplied so it's considered for interaction checking against the target.

Send every concurrently-taken medication you know about as
`current_medication` entries on the same request — Enterprise never reads a
saved list of current medications; it only sees what's on this request.

## Review behavior

Resolution results carry their own `review` state (`ReviewState`), separate
from a safety check's review state. A resolution that's ambiguous or
low-confidence may require review before you rely on the selected candidate
— check `review.required` on the resolution result, not just on a
downstream safety check.
